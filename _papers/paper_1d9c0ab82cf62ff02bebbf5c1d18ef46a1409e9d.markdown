: Large Audio Language Models (LALMs), which integrate understanding, reasoning, and generation across multiple audio domains, including speech, music, and environmental sounds, have emerged from advances in generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) tools and large language models (LLMs). However, the LALM field remains in its early stages, with a fragmented literature mostly focused on individual models or applications. This survey provides a thorough review of the LALM landscape, including design methodology, backbone audio representations, model architectures, training strategies, benchmark datasets, evaluation metrics, and main applications. Moreover, the authors propose a unified taxonomy based on architectural design, audio representation learning, modality alignment, and training paradigms, and compare the strengths, limitations, and trade-offs relevant to model deployment. The review of key applications covers audio foundation models for automatic speech recognition, speech translation, audio captioning, text-to-speech synthesis, and emerging audio reasoning tasks, together with the performance evaluation metrics used for each task. In addition, the survey examines key technical, computational, and ethical challenges, including hallucination, robustness, scalability, multilingual training, bias, privacy, and malicious misuse, and reviews known mitigation strategies and open research directions. This work uses a systematic literature selection strategy to improve transparency and reproducibility by summarizing representative works in the audio foundation model landscape. Finally, it outlines promising future directions toward efficient, trustworthy, and multimodal audio-language foundation models. This survey is intended as a comprehensive reference, providing researchers and practitioners with insights into the current state of the art in LALMs as well as future research opportunities in this domain.
