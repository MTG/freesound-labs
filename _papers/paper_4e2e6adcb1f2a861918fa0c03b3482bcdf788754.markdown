Compared to existing loop-detector or CCTV technologies, audio (even via mobile phones microphones) offers a cost-effective and scalable solution for vehicle detection and traffic monitoring (i.e., traffic count, speed estimation, etc.). This research provides a comparative analysis of the effectiveness of audio-based traffic monitoring under diverse acoustic situations (urban, suburban, noisy environments) through Machine Learning (ML) and Deep Learning (DL) models. The experiment utilized 41 audio features across 6 domains using Fast Fourier Transform (FFT), Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT), Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) and other transformation methods, along with 7 types of spectrograms and raw waveforms. A total of 16 different ML/DL methods, including Random Forest (RF) and Support Vector Machine (SVM), XGBoost and Extra Trees are examined. Experimental results on 3 benchmark datasets demonstrate the robustness and accuracy of ML method, with the RF achieving an accuracy of up to 99.8 % on the IDMT-traffic dataset. XGBoost and Extra Trees followed with accuracies of 97.5 % and 95.1 %, while CNN model using spectrograms achieved $\mathbf{7 8. 7 \% - 9 4. 7 \%}$ accuracy. These findings suggest that for simple tasks (traffic counting which is binary classification) ML models can outperform more complex DL models with significant time and resource savings. ML is effective when dealing with clear and consistent audio cues, such as regular engine sounds, and performs well even with smaller datasets due to its robustness and interpretability. In contrast, DL models are better suited for more complex tasks (distinguishing between acoustically similar vehicle types) by learning directly from raw waveforms. They can capture subtle and abstract patterns that extracted features may overlook, but typically require large amounts of labeled data.
